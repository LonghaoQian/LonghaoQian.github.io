I’m a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Toronto Institute for Aerospace Studies (UTIAS), working with Prof. Hugh H.T. Liu in the Flight Systems and Control (FSC) Lab. I build intelligent aerial robots: drones and drone-mounted manipulators that can perceive, reason, and physically interact with the world around them.

My work combines reinforcement learning, physics-informed neural networks with stability guarantees, and vision-language models, and I care about taking these methods out of simulation and onto real flight hardware. Recent projects include VLM-driven aerial grasping, contraction-based neural control, and cooperative drone navigation with UWB and LiDAR.

Before returning to UTIAS, I spent two years at Huawei as a chip software engineer, developing drivers and positioning algorithms for low-power ranging chips. I received my Ph.D. from UTIAS, where I worked on cooperative payload transportation with multiple drones, and I teach graduate courses on flight mechanics and UAV autonomy.

featured projects

Aerial Embodied Intelligence and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on a Drone-Manipulator Platform

L1 adaptive control for an aerial manipulator (3–4 cm average manipulation error), PPO sim-to-real correction, 400 Hz control from a Jetson Orin, and Qwen2.5-VL-driven autonomous grasping.

Trustworthy AI Control Framework Based on Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)

PINN-trained controllers with stability guarantees, generated without any training data; contraction metrics on Riemannian manifolds (RCCM) for singularity-free robust control. Accepted at CDC 2026 and AIAA SciTech 2027.

Cooperative UWB and LiDAR Drone Navigation with Reinforcement Learning (PPO)

Observability-Gramian-aware leader–follower formation flight where followers use only an IMU and a single UWB range; LiDAR SLAM for the leader and a PPO planning policy trained in Isaac Lab. Published in JGCD.
