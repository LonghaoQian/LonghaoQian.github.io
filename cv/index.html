Summary

I received my Ph.D. in aerospace engineering from the University of Toronto, Canada, and I am currently a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Toronto, focusing on aerial embodied intelligence for drones and the engineering deployment of AI algorithms. My core expertise spans reinforcement learning (PPO/MADDPG), physics-informed neural networks (PINNs), and multi-vehicle cooperative control, and I have full-stack R&D experience from algorithm design to hardware deployment, including real-time neural-network inference on NVIDIA Jetson Orin, Pixhawk flight-controller integration, and CAN-bus actuator control. I have led several complete projects through to deployment and validation: achieving 15 cm-level tracking accuracy in high-speed flight at 8–12 m/s, and proposing a novel scheduling algorithm for UWB chips that reduced system power consumption by 30%. I previously worked as a chip software engineer at Huawei Technologies Co., Ltd., where I became familiar with the R&D processes and coding standards of a large enterprise, and earned Huawei’s Professional-Level C++ Software Competency Certification and the HiSilicon Quality Star Award.

I have solid team-leadership experience. At the University of Toronto, I have led five R&D teams of 2–6 Ph.D., master’s, and undergraduate students, responsible for research planning, defining technical roadmaps, task allocation, and quality control of results, and I have guided these teams to publish in top journals such as JGCD and Science China and at top conferences such as CDC and AIAA SciTech. As lead of the control and visual-navigation subsystems for the A2RL international autonomous drone racing competition, I led the team to 7th place among 22 teams. At Huawei, I served as project lead and software Committer, led the team to complete driver development for a low-power ranging chip, and improved the team’s overall capability through regular technical training. I am skilled at coordinating cross-disciplinary R&D teams and efficiently driving the complete R&D cycle from solution design to engineering validation.

Project: Aerial Embodied Intelligence and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on a Drone-Manipulator Platform

2025/10 – present

  • Led a four-person team (3 Ph.D. students, 1 master’s student) to design interactive grasping and trajectory-tracking algorithms for a drone-manipulator platform. By analyzing the multibody dynamics equations of the manipulator platform, proposed an aerial embodied intelligence framework based on L1 adaptive control, filling the gap in theoretical stability proofs for aerial intelligent manipulator platforms. Achieved an average dynamic object-manipulation error of 3–4 cm in flight experiments — the highest accuracy for object manipulation with aerial serial manipulators reported in the existing literature. Implemented VLM capabilities for the aerial embodied platform based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Tongyi Qianwen), enabling the platform to autonomously recognize target objects and invoke the low-level visual localization algorithm for pre-grasp positioning; implemented natural-language control of the drone based on Qwen2.5-7B.
  • To address parameter uncertainties in the flight system — such as the effects of aerodynamic drag and propeller downwash (the downward airflow generated by the rotors) on flight — used Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) reinforcement learning combined with domain randomization to narrow the Sim2Real (simulation-to-reality transfer) gap. Trained the PPO control-correction algorithm in NVIDIA Isaac Sim 6.0 and Isaac Lab 3.0.
  • Deployed the embodied-intelligence control framework on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin onboard computer, with high-speed, low-latency UDP communication over Ethernet between the onboard computer and the flight control unit. Achieved, for the first time, direct control of the drone’s propellers and the manipulator by the NVIDIA Jetson Orin at 400 Hz, greatly improving manipulation and tracking accuracy.
  • Using XM430-W350 joint actuators, designed FOC (field-oriented control) current-loop control, improving torque actuation accuracy by about 20% in torque-control mode and laying the foundation for improving overall object-manipulation accuracy.
  • Using a RealSense depth camera and running YOLOv8 with Ultralytics, combined RGB object detection with depth information to achieve autonomous target recognition and localization, with centimeter-level localization accuracy for static targets.
  • Deployed the Qwen2.5-VL-7B large model locally on a ground-station computer equipped with an RTX 4070. The drone connects to the ground control station over a P2P network, which obtains the motion states of the drone and the manipulator in real time. By recognizing objects in camera images and invoking the precise localization results of the RealSense depth-camera module, the large model enables a fully autonomous environment-interaction system for the aerial embodied platform. The large model invokes the embodied control framework proposed in this research to command the drone to grasp and place objects, realizing a closed-loop physical-AI expert system centered on a large model.

Project: Trustworthy AI Control Framework Based on Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)

2025/08 – 2026/03

  • Led a two-person team (1 Ph.D. student, 1 master’s student) and proposed a nonlinear control training method based on physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) that enables neural networks to automatically generate robust control algorithms from a system’s multibody dynamics equations without any training dataset, enabling rapid generation of control schemes for complex systems. Compared with conventional machine-learning control, the PINN-generated controllers strictly satisfy the theoretical requirements of control stability, yielding reliable neural-network-based control.
  • Through differential geometry, combined PINNs with Riemannian manifold theory to achieve singularity-free control of the spatial attitude of drones and robots. Combined with contraction analysis control theory, transformed the robust control problem into the solution of a partial differential equation (PDE), and then solved the PDE with PINNs to obtain control networks with stability guarantees.
  • Proposed, for the first time, a robust control theory combining PINNs with Riemannian manifolds (Robust Control Contraction Metrics, RCCM), which still guarantees control accuracy in the presence of unknown external disturbances and parameter uncertainties.
  • The PINN-based AI control framework proposed in this research can be widely applied to the control of unmanned systems and embodied intelligence.
  • Results have been accepted by the top international control conference, the IEEE Conference on Decision and Control (CDC 2026), and by the AIAA 2027 SciTech Forum: “Neural Robust Control on Lie Groups Using Contraction Methods” (CDC 2026) and “Contraction-based Neural Control for Cooperative Aerial Payload Transportation with Variable-length Cables” (AIAA SciTech 2027).

Project: Cooperative UWB and LiDAR Drone Navigation with Reinforcement Learning (PPO)

2025/08 – 2026/03

  • Led a two-person team (1 Ph.D. student, 1 master’s student) and proposed a localization and navigation algorithm for drone formation flight based on the observability Gramian (a mathematical tool for measuring whether a system’s states can be accurately estimated).
  • Unlike conventional formation flight, this method only requires the leader to provide absolute position measurements via GPS or LiDAR SLAM; the followers rely only on their onboard IMU (inertial measurement unit) and a single range measurement from an ultra-wideband (UWB) system to estimate their 3D position relative to the leader, enabling close-proximity formation flight.
  • Theoretically analyzed the relationship between the observability Gramian and the follower’s localization error, and proposed an optimal leader–follower trajectory planning scheme: the leader and the follower move along specific trajectories so that the follower’s Kalman filter can use these trajectories to avoid observability singularities and achieve the minimum estimation error, filling a research gap on this problem. Results were published in the top international aerospace journal, the Journal of Guidance, Control, and Dynamics (JGCD): “Observability-aware control for quadrotor formation flight with range-only measurement” (2026).
  • Building on this theoretical scheme, developed a LiDAR-SLAM-based localization solution for the leader, enabling autonomous flight in GPS-denied environments.
  • Using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) reinforcement learning with the observability Gramian as part of the reward function, built a large-scale parallel reinforcement-learning training framework in NVIDIA Isaac Lab to obtain a reinforcement-learning formation-flight policy. The PPO-generated planning solves the difficulty that the observability-Gramian-based planning problem in the JGCD paper is hard to solve in real time, enabling the practical engineering deployment of this formation-flight scheme.

Project: Control of Aerial Slung-Load Manipulation Robots with Reinforcement Learning and Physics-Informed Neural Networks

2025/04 – 2026/06

  • Led a six-person team (3 Ph.D. students, 2 master’s students, 1 undergraduate) researching modeling, flight control, and trajectory planning algorithms for aerial slung-load manipulation robots based on reinforcement learning and physics-informed neural networks.
  • Proposed a nonlinear control training framework based on physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) that autonomously trains fault-tolerant control algorithms according to the stability principles of automatic control and, combined with the principle of robust disturbance observers, achieved for the first time multi-drone cooperative control of aerial slung-load manipulation robots under strong external disturbances.
  • Used Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) reinforcement learning combined with domain randomization to narrow the Sim2Real (simulation-to-reality transfer) gap.   • Combining PINNs with PPO control, deployed the neural networks on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin onboard computer using C++ and PyTorch, achieving for the first time PINN-based multi-drone cooperative control of aerial slung-load manipulation robots on a drone system.
  • Implemented a real-time payload-cable manipulation system based on CubeMars AK40-10 joint motors and a CAN communication system, and validated the proposed control algorithms on a quadrotor flight platform.
  • The results are currently under review: “A Robust Neural Control Design for Multi-drone Slung Payload Manipulation with Control Contraction Metrics” (arXiv:2510.01489), “Robust Anti-windup Tracking Control For A Pair of Quadrotors Carrying A Rigid-body Payload With Variable-Length Cable” (Automatica), and “Robust Neural Contraction Slung Load Manipulation With Active Cable Length Control” (JGCD).

Project: Reinforcement Learning for Drone Territory Security and Counter-UAV Interception

2024/09 – present

  • Led a three-person team (1 master’s student, 2 undergraduates) to design autonomous territory-defense interception algorithms for drones using reinforcement learning. Relying only on radio angle-of-arrival measurements of intruding drones, the algorithm plans the optimal task allocation and flight trajectories of the defending drones in real time, achieving autonomous swarm defense and interception.
  • Proposed an active localization and position-estimation strategy for illegally intruding drones based on MADDPG (Multi-Agent Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient). Assuming that the defending drones carry small onboard radars, trained optimal interception paths that maximize radar velocity-measurement and localization accuracy, improving the interception success rate. See “Investigating Multi-UAV Territory Guarding Using Cooperative-Competitive Reinforcement Learning” (AIAA SciTech 2026).
  • Building on that work, proposed an improved HA-RMAPPO-based reinforcement-learning scheme for drone-swarm territory defense, which uses measurements of the intruding drone’s radio return signals to achieve passive filtering and localization of illegally intruding drones. The scheme is suitable for rapid localization and interception of intruding drones by swarms of small, low-cost drones.
  • Building on conventional HA-RMAPPO, proposed an RNN-based training framework that, compared with an MLP architecture, improves the success rate of the final interception policy during training on the localization-and-interception problem. The final results were published in Science China Technological Sciences, a CAS Zone 1 top journal: “Hybrid reinforcement learning control for UAV territory guarding with cooperative angle-of-arrival localization” (2026).
